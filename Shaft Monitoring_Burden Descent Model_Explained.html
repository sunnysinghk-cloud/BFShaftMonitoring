2Acquire and align the data

Signals arrive at different speeds. The gateway subscribes to Level 1 by OPC UA, stamps every value with time and quality, and aligns everything on a common 5-second grid. Slow values (lab, configuration) are held until a new value arrives; events (a batch dump) keep their exact time.

x(tₖ) = last valid value with time ≤ tₖ,   tₖ = k × 5 sSample-and-hold onto the 5 s grid. Events are stored with their own time stamp, not rounded.
Example: stockrod 1 sends 1.43 m at 10:00:01, 1.44 m at 10:00:03 and 1.44 m at 10:00:05. The 10:00:05 grid point takes 1.44 m. The coke batch dump at 10:00:02.4 is kept as an event at exactly 10:00:02.4. The sinter bulk density from the lab (1.80 t/m³, measured last month) is held until the next lab value.

Minimum rates for M1: stockrods ≤ 5 s, pressures and blast ≤ 10 s, dumps and batch weights as events (≤ 1 s time stamp).

3Data quality: trust the signal before using it

Each value gets a quality flag before any KPI is calculated. A bad rod is removed from the average automatically and the operator sees why. In the animation rod 3 sticks; the model detects it and excludes it.

Stuck rod: rod speed = 0 for ≥ 3 min while the median rod speed ≥ 3 cm/min
Rod disagreement: |Lᵢ − median(L)| > 0.30 m for ≥ 5 min
Frozen value: identical value for > 60 samples (5 min at 5 s)
Spike: change > physical rate limit (e.g. > 1 m in 5 s without a dump or slip signature)
Rod status: rod lifting / not measuring (from PLC status bit) → value ignored
Example: at 10:12 rods 1 and 2 read 1.52 and 1.49 m and are falling at 9–10 cm/min. Rod 3 has read 1.18 m for 4 min. The stuck-rod rule fires, so rod 3 is excluded and the average stockline uses rods 1 and 2 only: (1.52 + 1.49) ÷ 2 = 1.505 m. Rod availability today: 22.8 h ÷ 24 h = 95 %.

4Stockline and burden descent rate

The stockrods sit on the burden surface. Between dumps the surface sinks as burden is consumed lower down, so the rods read deeper and deeper. Each dump raises the surface again, giving the saw-tooth. The model removes the jump at each dump and fits a slope inside each cycle: that slope is the descent rate.

5Hang and slip detection

Two rules watch the cleaned stockline together with top pressure and furnace status. Pick a scenario to see which rule fires.

Hang rule: v̄ < 1 cm/min on all valid rods for ≥ 5 min and wind on and no dump due
Slip rule: L̄ rises by ≥ 0.30 m within 30 s (burden drops) and top pressure spike ≥ +0.10 bar
Hang/slip frequency = number of events ÷ dayEach event is logged with time, rods, magnitude, duration and ΔP response. These records become the training labels for the ML early warning (step 10). Thresholds are agreed with operations.

6Gas flow resistance: ΔP and permeability index K

Gas has to push up through the burden. The harder it is, the larger the pressure drop for the same gas volume. K normalises the pressure drop by the gas volume, so it shows burden permeability independently of blast rate. Move the sliders.

ΔP = P_b − P_tHot blast pressure at the bustle main minus top pressure, both from Level 1.
V_bosh = 1.21 × V_air + 2 × V_O₂ + V_H₂Air: 21 % O₂ → 42 % CO, 79 % N₂ ⇒ 1.21 Nm³ bosh gas per Nm³ air. Each extra O₂ gives 2 CO. V_H₂ comes from blast moisture and PCI hydrogen (here 170 Nm³/min).
K = 10⁶ × (P_b,abs² − P_t,abs²) ÷ V_bosh^1.7Absolute pressures in bar (gauge + 1.013). The scaling constant and exponent are plant conventions; they are agreed with Client XXX and kept fixed.
Uptake avg = mean(T₁…T₄)  ·  Spread = T_max − T_minExample: 112, 121, 108, 142 °C → avg 120.8 °C, spread 34 °C. A rising spread means uneven gas flow (channelling).

7Charge volume and volumetric burden rate

Each batch weight comes from the weigh hoppers (Level 1 event). Dividing by bulk density from the lab gives the volume the charge takes in the furnace. Volume, not mass, is what makes the burden move down.

V_charge = Σ ( mᵢ ÷ ρᵢ )mᵢ = batch weight of material i (L1 weigh hopper). ρᵢ = bulk density (lab, t/m³).
V̇ = charges per hour × V_chargeVolumetric burden rate: the volume that must be consumed in the furnace every hour.

8Descent check: measured vs expected

If the burden is consumed at V̇ m³/h, the surface at the throat must sink by V̇ ÷ throat area. Comparing this with the measured descent shows hanging or abnormal consumption early, often before ΔP reacts.

A_throat = π × D_throat² ÷ 4From the furnace drawing (configuration).
v_expected = V̇ ÷ A_throat  ·  Deviation = (v̄_measured − v_expected) ÷ v_expected × 100

Persistent negative deviation (measured slower than expected) = burden held up (hanging tendency, scaffold). Persistent positive deviation = burden running faster than balance (check weights and densities, or a slip). Alert band agreed in shadow mode, e.g. ±8 %.

9Layer tracking: trajectory and time to tuyeres

Every charge becomes a layer at the stockline. As more charges are added on top and burden is consumed below, each layer moves down the furnace. Its depth comes from the furnace volume–height curve. The highlighted blue charges are a new recipe: watch the countdown to the tuyeres.

10Advisories and ML on top of the KPIs

The advisories reuse the KPIs already calculated, so they need little extra effort. Rule and statistical advisories start in Phase 1; the ML hang/slip warning starts in Phase 2, once enough labelled events have been collected.

Descent forecast: L̂(t+Δ) = L(t) + v̂ × Δ − Σ dump jumps scheduled in Δv̂ smoothed by a Kalman filter; 30-min forecast with 90 % band.
K deterioration (EWMA): zₜ = λ Kₜ + (1 − λ) zₜ₋₁   alert if zₜ > μ + 3σ √(λ ÷ (2 − λ))μ, σ from the last 7 stable days; λ ≈ 0.1.
Hang/slip risk: p = calibrated GBM(features)   advisory if p ≥ 30 %Features: K trend, ΔP rate, descent σ and asymmetry, uptake spread, deviation vs balance, blast changes, raw-material quality.
Recipe reminder: notify when ETA(first layer of new recipe) ≤ 30 min
Example: K rises from 2.85 to 3.06 in 40 min while asymmetry rises from 5 % to 12 %. EWMA z = 3.02 exceeds the alert limit 2.98, so an advisory is raised with a link to the permeability action standard. In Phase 2 the ML model also shows risk 34 %, with K trend and asymmetry as the top drivers.
